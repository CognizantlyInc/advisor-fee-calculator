Why it matters

Most investors pay two sets of fees, and see neither clearly.

One fee goes to the advisor. The other sits inside the funds. Statements don't add them up, and a rising balance looks like good performance even when much of it is your own savings.

Advisory fees show up on your statement. Fund expense ratios don't: they come out of returns before you ever see them. The natural question is whether the total paid for itself.

Starting with annual reports covering 2026, Canadian investors will see their total costs in dollars for the first time. The natural next question is whether those costs paid for themselves.

I

Was the investing any good?

Your returns before fees, compared with a low-cost benchmark matched to your mandate: an index fund, a 60/40 portfolio, or the policy benchmark in your Investment Policy Statement.

II

Was it worth the price?

Every cost in dollars, including the fees inside your funds, plus the growth those dollars would have earned. Then what today's fees mean over the next twenty years.

Why not just ask AI?

AI can do the arithmetic. A trustworthy answer takes more.

Go ahead and use it. The calculator above does the math for free. But the number is only as good as what goes into it, and a chatbot can't gather your records, choose a fair comparison, or stand behind the result.
