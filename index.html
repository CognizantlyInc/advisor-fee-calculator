Where do I find this?

Look for the performance page of your statement or online account. It's usually labeled "annualized return," "average annual return" or "time-weighted return," with 1-, 3-, 5- or 10-year figures. Use the figure for the whole account.

Your dealer must send an annual investment performance report. It shows your 1-, 3-, 5- and 10-year annualized returns, after fees. Those are money-weighted, which differs a little from the benchmark's figure if you made large deposits or withdrawals.

Returns are measured to December 31, so use your statement for the year ending December 31 if you can.

Why it matters

Most investors pay two sets of fees, and see neither clearly.

One fee goes to the advisor. The other sits inside the funds. Statements don't add them up, and a rising balance looks like good performance even when much of it is your own savings.

Advisory fees show up on your statement. Fund expense ratios don't: they come out of returns before you ever see them. The natural question is whether the total paid for itself.

Starting with annual reports covering 2026, Canadian investors will see their total costs in dollars for the first time. The natural next question is whether those costs paid for themselves.

I

Was the investing any good?

Your returns before fees, compared with a low-cost benchmark matched to your mandate: an index fund, a 60/40 portfolio, or the policy benchmark in your Investment Policy Statement.

II

Was it worth the price?

Every cost in dollars, including the fees inside your funds, plus the growth those dollars would have earned. Then what today's fees mean over the next twenty years.

Research on financial advice

What does the research say about financial advice?

Advisors play an important role. They get many households invested who would otherwise leave their savings in cash. Advised households tend to hold more of their savings in stocks, and that adds up over a lifetime.

The same research measures what the relationship costs, in fees and in returns that trail simple, low-cost alternatives. Putting numbers on both sides lets you decide, with clear information, whether your advice is worth what you pay.

Why not just ask AI?

AI can do arithmetic well, but a trustworthy answer takes expertise you can't prompt.

Don't take my word for it. Go ahead and use it. The calculator above does the math for free. But the number is only as good as what goes into it, and a chatbot can't gather your records, choose a fair comparison, or stand behind the result.
